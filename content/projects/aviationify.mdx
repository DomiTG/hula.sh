# Aviationify

> My first attempt at building a desktop application for the flight simulation community.

---

## The idea

One thing I always felt was missing from flight simulators was a sense of progression.

Flying from A to B is enjoyable, but after a while it becomes difficult to decide *where* to fly next. I wanted to build something that would give every flight a purpose instead of leaving every decision to the pilot.

That idea eventually became Aviationify.

<MdxImage src="/aviationify/jobs.webp" width={1920} height={1020}/>

The application generated an airline career where the user progressed through different airlines by completing scheduled flights. Routes were based on real-world operations and each completed flight contributed to the pilot's overall progression.

---

## Flight tracking

Aviationify communicated with Microsoft Flight Simulator through **FSUIPC**, allowing it to monitor the current flight in real time.

Using telemetry received from the simulator, the application could determine whether a flight had been completed successfully and automatically update the pilot's career without requiring manual input.

Communication between the desktop application and backend services was handled through WebSockets, making status updates immediate while the simulator was running.

---

## Technology

The entire application was built with **TypeScript** and **Electron**, making it possible to ship a native desktop experience while sharing the same language across the entire project.

Although the project only existed for about a month, it taught me a lot about desktop application development, realtime communication and integrating with external software that I didn't control.

---

## Looking back

Aviationify was never meant to become a finished product.

Instead, it became the foundation for everything I built afterwards.

Many of the ideas explored here eventually evolved into much larger projects with a broader scope, but this was where the journey started.
