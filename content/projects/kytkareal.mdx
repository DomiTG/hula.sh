## The first version

Kytka Real was one of my first commercial projects. The objective was to replace an outdated website with something that reflected the company's professionalism while keeping the experience simple for visitors.

The website introduced the agency, presented its services, displayed property listings and provided an easy way for potential clients to get in touch.

<MdxImage src="/kytkareal/v1.webp" width={1920} height={1020}/>

At the time, I was happy with the result.

Looking back today, I see many things I would approach differently.

---

## Looking back

After working on larger and more complex projects, my standards changed.

I became much more interested in things like:

- visual hierarchy
- spacing and typography
- accessibility
- maintainability
- reusable components
- overall user experience

None of these were *bad* in the original version, but I knew they could be significantly better.

Instead of continuing to build on top of an architecture I had outgrown, I decided to start over.

---

## The redesign

The current version is **not** just a new coat of paint.

Almost every page has been redesigned from scratch.

<MdxImage src="/kytkareal/v2.webp" width={1920} height={1020}/>

The redesign focuses on creating a calmer interface with more whitespace, improved readability and a more consistent design system. Behind the scenes, the component structure has also been reorganised to make future development easier.

Although the redesign is still in progress, the direction of the project is already visible.

You can preview it here:

**https://kytkareal-cz.vercel.app**

---

## Work in progress

The redesign is still under active development.

Some pages are unfinished, details continue to change and new sections are added regularly. The preview exists to document the current progress rather than represent the final product.

---

## What I learned

Projects like this are interesting because they show progress over time.

The original website represents the developer I was in 2024.

The redesign represents how I approach projects today.

Rather than trying to preserve every decision I made in the past, I prefer revisiting older work, identifying weak points and rebuilding them properly. I think that process says much more about me as a developer than keeping every old project untouched.
